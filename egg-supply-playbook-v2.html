The egg trading
playbook

A guide to buying eggs at ₹8, selling at ₹10, and finding where the real money is. It explains each term as it comes up, so you don't need to know the egg business to follow it.

The short answer

At ₹8 in and ₹10 out, a ₹2 gap is not enough to run a hired-help business at about 1,350 eggs a day. It loses roughly ₹64,000 a month. Run by the owner with almost no overhead it breaks even. The money is in four moves: buy nearer ₹7.50, sell nearer ₹10.50 with graded and clean eggs, add a small genuine country-egg line, and grow to about 3,000 eggs a day.

01

Five findings

Everything else in this report supports these five points.

Finding 1

₹2 isn’t enough

Costs eat ₹0.45 an egg in breakage and packing. Labour, transport, rent and testing eat another ₹1.50–3.10. Run lean, you break even. Hire help, you lose about ₹64,000 a month.

Finding 2

You may overpay for eggs

₹8 is ₹1 over today’s ₹7.00 wholesale reference. A fair brown premium is probably ₹0.50–0.75. That is an estimate. Get three quotes.

Finding 3

Stay with brown eggs

Brown strains such as BV380 give the best margin you can actually supply every day. Real country eggs pay more per egg but only come in a few hundred a day.

Finding 4

Selling is about the buyer

Distributors and restaurants pay about ₹8–9.50. Brands, shops and premium buyers can pay ₹10.50 and up for graded, clean, tested eggs.

Finding 5

Volume is the lever

Overhead per egg falls from ₹3.12 at 1,350 a day to ₹2.13 at 3,000. At tuned prices (buy ₹7.60, sell ₹10.50) a hired-help business at 3,000 a day makes about ₹29,000 a month.

Warning

Don’t use “country”

Authorities seize dyed and mislabelled eggs. Over 80,000 were seized in Moradabad in Nov 2025. Sell BV380 as “farm-fresh brown eggs”, nothing more.

Opportunity

Put the five findings together and the business changes from “a trader at ₹2 a egg” into “a small aggregator with a premium-quality offer”.

  • Today, running lean: about ₹2,000 a month.
  • With tuned buying and selling prices, still lean: about ₹38,000 a month.
  • Add a genuine country-egg line and a small boiled-egg pilot: about ₹50,000–65,000 a month. The pilot is unproven.
02

Egg business basics

Skip this section if you know the trade. If you are new, it explains every term used later.

In plain words

An egg business moves one product through five hands, and each hand takes a cut. You sit in the middle as a trader: you buy from farms, check quality, and sell to shops, brands and kitchens.

How an egg gets from farm to plate

1 · Farm

Hens lay

A farm keeps thousands of hens. Eggs are collected daily. Typical farm cost is about ₹5–5.50 an egg, mostly feed.

2 · You

Trader

Buy from several farms, check quality, collect and deliver. Buying ₹8, selling ₹10.

3 · Brand or packer

Grade and pack

Sorts by size, cleans, puts in cartons, adds a brand and a label.

4 · Shop or app

Retail

BigBasket, Zepto, supermarkets and kirana stores. They add a large margin.

5 · Customer

Pays shelf price

About ₹10.80–25 an egg depending on the type of egg.

The four kinds of egg you will hear about

White
₹7–8

From white-feathered commercial hens. This is what the daily wholesale price covers. Cheapest and the biggest volume. Bakeries and hostels buy it.

Brown (yours)
₹10–13

From commercial brown hens such as BV380. Many buyers wrongly think brown means country. Priced a little above white at wholesale.

Country / nati
₹15–18

From backyard or village hens. Smaller eggs, far fewer per hen. Retail prices are 2–3 times white. A magnet for fakes.

Premium
₹16–30

Omega-3, cage-free, free-range, organic. Mostly produced by brands with their own farms, not by traders.

Ranges are retail prices per egg. Sources are app and store listings of various dates. Wholesale prices are lower.

Glossary

NECC
National Egg Coordination Committee, a farmers’ body. It announces a suggested wholesale price per egg every day for each zone. It covers white eggs only. On 5 Nov 2026 Bengaluru was ₹7.00.
BV380
A brown-egg hen from Venkateshwara Hatcheries. It is a commercial hybrid, not a country bird.
Layer
A hen kept for eggs, not meat. A good commercial layer gives about 300 eggs a year.
Ungraded / graded
Ungraded eggs are mixed sizes. Graded eggs are sorted by weight, from small (38–44 g) to extra large (60 g and up).
Candling
Holding an egg to a bright light to spot cracks and defects. A cheap LED candler costs about ₹1,650.
Dry cleaning
Wiping dirt off the shell with a dry cloth or pad. Washing is avoided because it strips the shell’s natural coating and shortens shelf life.
FSSAI
The food safety regulator. Every food seller needs a registration or licence.
GST nil-rated
Fresh eggs carry no GST. Buyers may still ask for a GST number.
HoReCa
Hotels, restaurants and catering. They buy eggs in large quantities, mostly on price.
Quick commerce
Delivery apps such as Blinkit, Zepto and Swiggy Instamart. They sell brands, not loose eggs from traders.
White-label / private label
Eggs sold under the shop or app’s own name, made by someone else. Relish (Zepto) and Fresho (BigBasket) are examples.
Working capital
Money tied up in eggs you have paid for but not yet been paid for. It is the cash you must have on hand to keep trading.
Overhead
Costs you pay even if you sell one egg more or less: labour, rent, vehicle, lab tests, accounting.
Contribution / gross margin
Selling price minus the cost of the eggs and packing, before overhead.
Break-even
The volume or price at which profit is exactly zero.
Residue test
A lab test that checks eggs for banned antibiotics. Buyers now ask for it after a 2025 scare.
Hen-housed eggs
Eggs per hen counted from the day she is placed, including hens that die. The fair way to compare breeds.
AGMARK
A voluntary government grading and quality mark for eggs.

Prices: where ₹8 and ₹10 sit

First, what the egg market actually pays, then where your ₹8 buying price and ₹10 selling price fall in it.

In plain words

There is one published daily egg price in India, and it is for white eggs. Brown eggs have no published price. They sell for “the white price plus a bit”, and that bit is agreed between buyer and seller. The “bit” is the thing to negotiate.

Opportunity

Because buyers don’t all pay the same, the buyer you pick matters as much as the egg you sell.

  • Stay out of the cheap end. Hostels and caterers buy white eggs at about ₹7. You cannot win there with ₹8 eggs.
  • Aim at the ₹10.50–12 range: shops, bakeries and small brands that want graded, clean, traceable eggs. Energy Eggs in Hoskote sells brown wholesale at ₹12.
  • Price off the market. Quote “NECC + ₹3” with ₹10 as the floor. In peak months you earn more; in low months you still earn.

What one egg earns, what the month earns, and what it takes to make the business pay.

In plain words

You earn ₹2 on every egg, but that ₹2 has to pay for breakage, trays, the helper, transport, rent and lab tests. Add those up per egg and they are close to ₹2 already. The only way to make real money is to shrink those costs, widen the ₹2, or spread the costs over more eggs.

Which breed gives better numbers?

You don’t keep hens, so “breed” here means which type of egg to buy and sell. The short answer: stay with brown, add a small genuine country line.

In plain words

A commercial hen is bred to lay almost an egg a day, for about 18 months. A country hen lays a third to half as many. Fewer eggs means each one costs the farm more, and supply is scattered across villages. That is why country eggs sell for two to three times as much, and why you can only get a few hundred a day.

Why a farm can accept less than ₹8

Feed is the biggest cost. A BV380 hen eats about 139 g of feed per egg, roughly ₹4.20–4.40 an egg at ₹30–32 a kg. Total cost on a white-egg farm in Namakkal was about ₹4.50–5.20 an egg in 2025–26 (published, two sources). A brown-egg farm costs a little more. At ₹8 the farm earns about ₹2.50 an egg. That is a lot, and it is the room you have to negotiate.

What it means for brown strains

To a buyer, all brown eggs are the same. Nobody pays more for a BV380 egg than for a Hy-Line Brown or a Lohmann Brown egg. Imported strains lay slightly bigger eggs, which grade better. They matter only if you can find farms that keep them, which is hard near Bangalore.

Fake country eggs. Many “country” eggs listed at ₹6 are really commercial brown eggs. Coimbatore officials seized about 3,900 dyed eggs in 2020, and Moradabad over 80,000 in Nov 2025. If you add a country line, buy only from farms you have visited, and say so honestly on the label.
Opportunity

What changing the type of egg is worth, at your volume.

  • Stay brown for the main business. It earns ₹1.40–1.70 per egg before overhead, about ₹1,700–2,500 a day. White earns only ₹360–1,500 a day.
  • Add 150–300 genuine country eggs a day. Roughly +₹1–3 an egg, so +₹4,500 to +₹27,000 a month.
  • Chicks for country breeds (Giriraja, Swarnadhara) come from Karnataka’s veterinary university (KVAFSU). You could pay a village farmer’s group to raise them for you, then buy their eggs. The university sells chicks, not eggs.
  • Add white eggs only as a volume line for bakeries. It brings you customers but little profit.

Your best sales route is one that moves up as your eggs get better.

08

Rules, labels and documents

What you must have, what you may say on a label, and what buyers ask for first.

In plain words

The law lets you sell eggs with a basic registration. It is strict about what you say on the label: every claim must be true and provable. “Country” and “free-range” have no legal definition, so you need proof. Only “organic” needs a certificate.

The December 2025 scare

A viral test reported a banned antibiotic trace (AOZ, 0.73 µg/kg) in Eggoz eggs. FSSAI ordered nationwide sampling, with more than 150 Karnataka samples, then on about 20 December called the cancer claims misleading. No final results were published. Buyers now ask suppliers for residue reports.

What buyers ask for first

Legal and onboarding

0 / 0
  • FSSAI registration as a trader. Some buyers want a State licence.
  • GSTIN. Not legally needed for eggs, but nearly every buyer asks. File nil returns.
  • PAN and a current bank account with a cancelled cheque.
  • Udyam (MSME) registration. Free.
  • Shop and establishment or trade licence (₹500–3,000) and address proof.
  • Signed vendor form and supplier code of conduct when a buyer sends them.

Quality and proof

0 / 0
  • Residue report per farm from an NABL lab: nitrofuran metabolites (AOZ, AMOZ, AHD, SEM) and chloramphenicol, plus Salmonella. About ₹8,850 a sample at government rates.
  • Farm list: names, places, flock sizes, breed, flock age, vaccination and vet records.
  • Signed “no banned antibiotics” declaration from each farm, with feed details.
  • QC sheet: weight grade, crack limit, days since laying, shell cleanliness, candling check.
  • Traceability record linking each lot to its farm and laying date.
  • Dry-cleaning method written down.

Commercial terms

0 / 0
  • Price sheet by size grade, ex-farm and delivered.
  • Committed weekly capacity and a backup plan: 3–5 farms in your pool.
  • Packing: 30-egg trays or crates, lot number and laying date on the label.
  • Delivery: days, time slots, a closed vehicle kept cool.
  • Breakage and returns policy and the credit period you accept.
  • Sample lot: 5–10 trays, then a 2–4 week trial order.

What to say on the label

Safe: “Farm-fresh brown eggs. Traceable. Residue-tested. Delivered within 48 hours of laying.” Say “residue-tested” only once you hold a lab report.

Not safe: country, desi, nati, free-range, organic, omega-3 or any other claim you cannot prove for BV380 eggs.

Running it day to day

Where to buy, how to move eggs, how long they last, and when to lock prices.

In plain words

Eggs are fragile and perishable. They keep about a week in a hot Bangalore summer and ten days in winter, at room temperature. So you buy close, move fast, and don’t stockpile.

Buy from nearby, not far

Trucking eggs from Namakkal or Hospet costs ₹0.95–1.20 an egg in freight with a hired small truck. The price gap to Bangalore is only ₹0.50–0.60. Those zones also supply mostly white eggs. Better: a daily 60–120 km round of 3–6 farms in Kolar, Chikkaballapur, Hosur, Tumakuru and Ramanagara.

Checking a farm before you buy

  • Flock age. 20–24 weeks: small eggs. 28–55 weeks: best. 60+ weeks: big, pale, thin-shelled. Ask the chick placement date.
  • Medicine log. Don’t buy during treatment or the withdrawal period.
  • Feed. Own-mixed or branded? Is calcium added?
  • Size. Weigh a sample of 30 eggs per lot.
  • Shell. Candle 30 eggs and record cracks and colour.
  • Cost: ₹3,000–7,000 for scale and candler, plus 1–3 paise an egg for lab tests (estimates).

Bird flu cases in Karnataka (including near Bengaluru in April 2026) can pause demand for weeks.

Risks and open questions

Price swings

A fixed ₹8 buying price against a market that moves ₹2.60 a year can erase your margin in a crash. Link the price to NECC.

Cash tied up

Platforms, brands and chains pay in 15–60 days. One report put Zepto’s payments owed to merchants and suppliers at about ₹3,725 crore as of March 2026.

Residue compliance

After the December 2025 scare, buyers ask for lab reports. Nitrofurans are banned in poultry. Don’t buy from a farm that won’t show its medicine log.

Fake or mislabelled eggs

Authorities seize dyed and mislabelled eggs. Never label BV380 as country. If you add a country line, buy only from farms you’ve visited.

Small volume

1,350 eggs a day is too small for Eggoz, Suguna and the national chains. It fits traders, bakeries and mid-sized brands.
